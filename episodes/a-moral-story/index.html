A Moral Story

26 November 2026·70 min·with Yuriy Boyechko

Yuriy Boyechko, founder and CEO of Hope for Ukraine and author of the forthcoming book Defiant Hope, joins Kim Kucinskas, Thomas Jepson-Lay and Ali Al Mokdad. Ali starts with the numbers: about $5 million in revenue, around 90% of it going to programmes, and millions of people reached. Yuriy's answer is operations: around 1,500 volunteers on the ground, vendors reassessed every week, food bought from Ukrainian producers, and an app his team built, "DoorDash for humanitarian aid", that tracks aid from the warehouse to the distribution. He talks about 2023, when revenue dropped 75% in one year, and the decision to innovate, with programming built around what is needed most on the ground, from a solar energy programme that began with one generator bought at Costco to rescuing abandoned pets. He also tells the story of Natalia in Kherson, whose answer to every suggestion to leave is the same: if not us, then who? Thomas on how the international system rolled into Ukraine with the same model it uses everywhere else, and on the difference between scale and replicability. Kim on the intersection between impact and operations, and on whether everything needs to scale. Ali on energy as humanitarian assistance, a type of programming the sector still does not fully embrace.
